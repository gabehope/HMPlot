Examples

Choose a chart to open its live preview and source. Examples mount individually.

Ten million stars, filtered live

Ten million synthetic stars generated by Data(rows) on the GPU, age-filtered live, with a fixed 100,000-star H–R sample that brushes the whole galaxy. Square points and additive starlight.

Mixing in a double gyre

45,000 particles ride the double gyre, a standard model of mixing in ocean flows, stepped on the GPU by Simulate at the display's rate. Each is colored by where it started, and the wobble of the eddies' boundary folds the two into each other; a Shift-drag box drops dye that the particles carry downstream in thin filaments (the brush feeds the simulation's state). A slider sets the wobble: at 0 the eddies never mix.

A globe with streamed map tiles

An orthographic globe whose basemap streams in as you turn and zoom it: OpenStreetMap vector tiles from VersaTiles, loaded at the zoom the view needs, from the whole planet down to a city's streets, with their credit in the corner. Every magnitude 4.5+ earthquake of 2024 (USGS) sits on top, sized by magnitude and colored by depth.

Lorenz attractor

Sixty thousand Runge-Kutta steps of the Lorenz system trace its butterfly; on the GPU every point then moves on at the Rayleigh number you drag, collapsing onto two fixed points below about 24.7. A glowing 3D orbit with sparks that run along it in time order (evaluated on the GPU from @time), linked to the butterfly projection: brush a region and the 3D view keeps only those points lit while a speed histogram counts them.

Earthquakes beneath the globe

Every earthquake of magnitude 4.5+ in 2024 (6,397 events, USGS) placed at its true depth inside a see-through globe, where deep quakes trace subducting slabs; a slider exaggerates the depths. Brush a region on the map and a period on the timeline; the brushes combine, the globe keeps the matching quakes lit, and a log-scale histogram shows the Gutenberg–Richter law.

Calculated scatterplot matrix

Sixteen coordinated views of four calculated fields. Hover, select, and Shift-drag to brush across cells. Counts are per cell; 25,000 rows produces 400,000 plotted instances.

Large calculated point cloud

Three sequential Calculate scopes share intermediate columns. Change gain to update the final stage; hover, select, and Shift-drag to brush the resulting point cloud. No sampling limit is applied.

Coordinated interaction dashboard

Hover either shared view: the same row enlarges in both. Click/Shift-click selects/toggles; Shift-drag brushes. Drag the brush or click away to clear it. The filtered view updates while brushing. The independent table must not react, even though its IDs and values match.

Interaction matrix and scrolling

The same observation appears in all 16 cells. Hover should enlarge it everywhere; selection and brushing should coordinate by row, not category. Scroll away and back, then repeat. Compare cache sizes 0 and 64. Pan and zoom are independent in this example.

Keyed updates under interaction

Select a few points, hover, then step the frame or reverse the rows. Surviving IDs retain selection; removed IDs disappear. Point fades last 500 ms and label fades last 200 ms. No automatic timer runs: every update is explicit. Hover size is immediate unless the configured animation applies.

Additional examples

Marks

Symlog axis and a diverging scale

Values that cross zero with a wide range: a symlog y axis is linear within ±10 and logarithmic beyond, so small days and large ones both read, and outflows are drawn (a log axis would drop them). The color scale's midpoint puts RdBu's white at 0 and uses both ends of the colormap.

A fitted curve that refits under a brush

Smooth fits a curve (linear, polynomial, log, exp or power) with its 95% confidence band on the GPU: penguins' body mass against flipper length. Brush some penguins and the fit follows them.

ECDFs by species

Each penguin species' body mass as steps: the fraction of its penguins at or below each mass.

Density contours

The density of penguins' bill length against depth as filled bands, contour lines or both, on the GPU: Density, then Contour's marching squares.

Voronoi cells, hulls and a Delaunay triangulation

Each penguin's Voronoi cell (hover one to highlight its penguin), each species' convex hull, and the Delaunay triangulation, drawn by Polygon and Segments.

Box plot

Quartiles, Tukey whiskers, medians, and source-linked outliers.

Swarm plot

Deterministic glyph-aware dodge with stable observation identities.

Bar chart

Categorical bars composed from derived geometry and instanced prisms.

Streamgraph

Stack with offset="wiggle" and order="insideOut": each week's stack moves so the layers change slope as little as they can, the biggest genres in the middle, and an Area per genre draws it. The controls switch the offset and the order. Area transitions animate by default; the Animate control can turn them off.

100% stacked bars

Stack with offset="expand" scales each region's stack to span 0 to 1, so each Bar segment is a product's share of the region's sales.

Composition

Portable candlestick template

OHLC candles composed from ErrorBars, Calculate, Filter and Rect. Download the template and supply your own price table.

Portable parallel coordinates

Fold and Normalize turn a wide table into comparable dimensions. A portable Line template draws one profile per observation.

Portable ridgeline template

Grouped Gaussian densities in one shared frame, packaged as a downloadable template. Change the data, bandwidth and ridge height.

Portable radar template

A portable polygon template maps normalized scores to a radar chart. Rings, spokes and labels are supplied by the host.

Ridgeline temperatures

Twelve monthly temperature densities from Lincoln's 2016 weather, with overlapping ridges and a shared blue-to-red temperature gradient.

Embedded facets

Facets inside a Plot partition its frame and share its renderer and camera. For grouped bars, use Bar with arrange="group".

Waffle grid

Integer counts expand into source-identified unit squares, ordered left to right and bottom to top.

Nested scales

A child interval maps through its parent’s logarithmic scale.

Anchored frame

Pan and zoom: the child frame stays 160 by 100 CSS pixels, anchored at its parent data position.

Titles, bands and callouts

A plot's title, subtitle and caption are part of the plot and its exports; bands shade a span of an axis behind the marks and grid; callouts point at a value with a note.

Marks in nested frames

Area, ErrorBars, Rect, and curved Segments inside a linear child of a log parent, and bars with error bars in an anchored inset that keeps its pixel size while you pan and zoom.

One brush, three strips

Three child frames plot score against different fields. Shift-drag any tick strip: the shared score brush appears on every strip. Drag band ends to resize, the middle to move, scroll to scale, and use arrow keys or Escape.

Legend channels

Colored symbols when one column drives fill and shape, a size ramp for a numeric column, and one legend for a facet grid that hides a species in every cell.

RGB colors from data

Color each point from red, green, and blue columns. Integer data is 0-255 and float data 0-1; RGB colors bypass scales and legends.

Custom colormaps

A diverging colormap from three colors and a positioned ramp that saves its strongest colors for the top of the domain. Legends and exports follow.

Custom point shapes

Register polygons as point shapes and map a column to them with shape and shapeScale; the legend and SVG export draw the same polygons.

Mona Lisa histogram

Every pixel of the painting is a data-space square with RGB fill, and an RGB histogram sits over its lower part, its three channels screen-blended so overlaps add up to white as in the D3 notebook. Brush a region and the histogram counts only those pixels. After the D3 notebook of the same name.

animate, enter/exit, and @time

animate() moves one channel per row in its scale space (geometric on this log axis) with enter and exit values for rows that come and go, while @time satellites on fixed rows of their own orbit continuously without blocking settlement.

tween and @time in the pipeline

A tweened column moves as data: the histogram under it re-bins its rows every frame while they glide to new values, and a Filter on @time lets points in as the view clock passes them.

One density per group, one frame

Partition repeats its children once per group inside a single frame, sharing the parent's scales, axes, and legend. Here a Density transform runs per species so the curves overlay; Facet would give each species its own cell instead.

Lollipop chart

A stem from the baseline to each value, on either side of it, with a point at the end. Lollipop is a built-in template: BarGeometry places each stem, Segments draws the stems and Points the heads.

Pie chart

Each region's share of the sales, clockwise from 12 o'clock, labeled with its percentage. Pie is a built-in template: a PieGeometry transform and Rects in a polar Frame, which it places in a Cartesian Plot.

Donut chart

innerRadius opens the hole and padAngle separates the sectors. Keyed rows tween each sector's angles when the values change.

Pies in a Facet

Small multiples: a Facet repeats the Plot once per year, and each Pie sums its own year's sales, with one legend for the row.

Before and after arrows

connect() pairs two marks' rows by key and draws one arrow per pair. The 2000 values are points, the 2021 values an invisible Anchor, and the arrows take their color from the shared table.

Parallel coordinates from connections

An embedded Facet with fields makes one cell per measurement, in which value is that measurement, each with its own y scale titled by the measurement, and a Frame in every cell sets x from 0 to 1, so the points sit on each cell's axis. One Line connects the points across the cells in facet order, and every cell shows the same penguins, so rows match without a key. Shift-drag on any axis to brush that measurement; brushes on several axes combine.

One cell per variable

Facet fields repeats its children once per field over the same rows; inside each cell, value is that field. Each field keeps its own y scale while columns of the table stay shared. With row="station" the fields form a grid by station, a field keeping one scale down its column.

Interaction

Particles without an uploaded table

Data(rows) generates row indices, ones and fixed random values on the GPU; Simulate moves the particles without a dummy table from Python.

Brushed filter chain

Shift-drag to brush 50,000 points; a histogram (Filter > Bin) and per-level means (Filter > Group) follow the brush. A Filter keeps its input's rows and marks the kept ones, so a moving brush changes no array length and compiles no new GPU kernels.

Running totals under a brush

Shift-drag to brush 50,000 transactions; the brushed rows are sorted by day, then a running sum and a 500-row moving average follow the brush (Filter > Sort > Window > Line).

Brushed distributions

Shift-drag to brush 60,000 points in five clusters; violins and box plots of each group's values are recomputed from the brushed rows on every move, with no GPU kernels compiled while dragging.

Moving particles

1,500 particles circle at their own speeds: Slice interpolates every particle's position at the current time on every animation frame, and a slider filters them by speed. Neither playing nor filtering compiles new GPU kernels once warm.

Half a million points, one slider

A slider filters 500,000 points by magnitude into a 3D hexbin and a histogram. A Filter that keeps under a quarter of a large input packs the kept rows into a few fixed lengths, so the work after it follows the selection and each length compiles once.

Nearby points

Move the pointer over the clusters: every row within the radius grows, including all overplotted rows at a cluster center, and the rest shrink. Adjust the radius with the slider.

Crosshair and rules

Move the pointer over a time series: the crosshair follows it with a readout of each value. A dashed rule marks a threshold and gray rules mark events from a second table, labeled at the top of the frame.

Tooltips

Point near a penguin: the tooltip shows the nearest one within 16 pixels, with chosen fields, labels and formats, and @tooltip enlarges it; a histogram's bars show their bin and count.

Unified x tooltip

Move the pointer along the dates: one tooltip lists every series' value at that date, with a marker on each line, beside a Crosshair's vertical rule.

Nearby along x

Single-axis nearby in data units picks every series at the day under the pointer, whatever the pointer's height, and labels them.

Respond only while active

@any_brushed and @any_selected are 1 on every row while anything is brushed or selected, so the other rows shrink only then. Shift-drag or click in either plot; clear to restore every row.

Joining two tables

Flights name their origin airport by code; Join adds each airport's region from a second table, so the flights are colored, grouped on an axis and brushed by it.

Dragging points

Drag a point in a scatterplot matrix: the drag writes offsets on the table's rows, and every cell that reads moved() of the dragged column follows.

Dragging cities on a map

Drag a city on a Mercator map: the drag goes back through the projection and writes offsets in degrees; the point and its label both read moved().

Time

Calendar functions and a Playback over dates

Mauna Loa's monthly CO2: a Playback whose value is a date draws it a month at a time, four years a second, and month_name groups the seasonal cycle by calendar month.

Maps

Earthquakes from a Parquet file

A Provider reads three of a Parquet file's five columns (ten years of USGS earthquakes, magnitude 4.5+) with HTTP range requests; the other two are never downloaded. Python reads the same columns with hm.Table.from_parquet.

Earthquakes by year, loaded on demand

A Provider loads one year of real earthquakes (USGS, magnitude 4.5+) from a static CSV when the Year input changes. The previous year stays drawn until the next one arrives, a year already on screen loads nothing, and the Refresh button loads it again.

Vector tiles that follow the view

A VectorTile loads the tile named for a place at full detail, then, as you pan and zoom, the tiles the view shows (OpenStreetMap data as Mapbox Vector Tiles from VersaTiles), each drawn as soon as it arrives: water, parks, buildings, roads, railways and place names. Pick a place, a theme to recolor the basemap, hide layers (a hidden layer is never decoded), or turn Live off to keep only the named tile; the credit line is part of the plot and its exports.

A gridded field on the globe, month by month

A grid table (month x latitude x longitude, one row per cell) holds a made-up surface temperature on 48 uneven latitudes. Slice through time blends the two months around a Playback's value on the GPU; the Heatmap places the latitudes by their coordinates.

A Hovmöller diagram

Slice at one latitude of a time x latitude x longitude grid table leaves time against longitude: an anomaly travelling east is diagonal banding. The latitude is a slider.

An RGB image from band columns

An Image's fill takes { r, g, b } columns, as every mark's RGB channel does: three satellite-style bands of a grid table (integers 0-255) make the image, placed by the grid's coordinates.

World map projections

equal-earth and natural-earth world maps, albers (conic equal-area) and albers-usa (the United States with Alaska and Hawaii as insets): each maps longitude and latitude jointly into one curved outline around its center, cut at its seam, with a graticule and outline instead of edge axes. Move the center to move the seam; click a country for the longitude and latitude under the pointer.
